# Race control, racecraft and aero — 29 October 2026

## Scope and source research
This game retains a 2025-style Formula ruleset with DRS, not 2026 active aero. The current 2026 driving guidance was also reviewed for continuity. FIA guidance is interpretive, not the regulations themselves. The game's decisions are deterministic approximations; they are not a claim to reproduce human stewards or EA's proprietary algorithms.

The [2025 FIA driving guidelines](https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/f1_driving_standards_guidelines_version_4.1_feb_20_2025.pdf) distinguish track limits from lasting advantage, describe exceptions for loss of control/avoidance/being forced off, require safe rejoining, prohibit repeated defensive moves and defensive direction changes during braking, and set different inside/outside overtaking entitlement criteria. The [2026 guidance](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/2026_f1_driving_standards_guidelines.pdf) also stresses context and discretion when an unsuccessful pass gains no advantage. Exact intent is not observable from a brake button.

The [2025 FIA penalty guidance](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/2025_f1_guidelines_penalty_points_overview_-_14_may_clean_0.pdf) lists a final warning on the third ordinary track-limit strike, five seconds for the fourth and subsequent strikes, and a ten-second baseline for lasting advantage, with five seconds for mitigation. This informed the game's warning ladder and separate advantage review.

[EA's F1 25 telemetry specification](https://forums.ea.com/t5/s/tghpe58374/attachments/tghpe58374/f1-games-game-info-hub-en/61/4/Data%20Output%20from%20F1%2025%20v3.pdf) exposes distinct corner-cutting time-gain, off-track overtake, failure-to-return-position, and minor/significant/extreme running-wide events. It does not publish the classification thresholds or an intent estimator. These categories are evidence of separate outcomes, not a source for invented EA coefficients.

[2025 Sporting Regulations, article 22](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2025_formula_1_sporting_regulations_-_issue_5_-_2025-04-30.pdf) restrict DRS to activation zones, enable race use after one completed lap, require less than one second at detection, and close the system on braking. [FIA Silverstone circuit notes](https://www.fia.com/news/british-grand-prix-preview) locate detection around Village and Maggotts, with activation after Aintree and Chapel. The game's metre coordinates are conservative placements on the imported model, not surveyed FIA coordinates.

## Player and AI performance
Player Formula downforce is 2.8, compared with the original 2.6: about 7.7% more (a 3.7% increase over the previous 2.7 revision) at equal speed and slip. Steering range is restored to the original .032 base. Power is unchanged. Whether Copse is barely flat on a particular line still needs human driving validation, including entry speed and exit road geometry. No claim that two wheels must land at a precise location.

Equal cars is the default. Optional Enhanced AI uses the same force solver with an explicitly stronger vehicle: at 100% skill, 70% more downforce, 5% more drive force/power, and 28% more available steering range. The advantage scales with the cube of skill; it never changes player settings or depends on the player's position. No rubber-banding. AI speed profiles use the enhanced vehicle envelope, while the browser reuses the precomputed path. The stronger car still obeys tyre-force limits and penalties. Enhanced performance remains experimental: it can feel unfair even when its lap times are competitive. Equal cars is recommended.

## Race-control implementation
`race-steward.js` is shared by solo races, AI and the authoritative multiplayer server. Practice/time-trial lap validation remains separate. The chosen boundary policy applies to practice and races. Private rooms inherit the host choice: normal road edges, low kerbs included, roughly one metre beyond those boundaries, or no track-limit sanctions. This changes legality, not surface grip.

An excursion begins at four wheels outside the sampled racing surface. Boundary flickers under 0.1 seconds are ignored for race sanctions; rejoining for half a second merges brief edge bounces into one incident. Lap invalidation can still occur independently. The evaluator records route advance, actual distance, estimated legal travel time, braking, throttle, loss of control, recent contact and nearby rivals at departure/rejoin.

Geometric shortcut gain and a conservative corner-speed timing estimate detect time advantage. Position changes detect an off-track overtake even without a shortcut. A close defensive cut can also require handing back a position. The estimate is deliberately approximate, not a precise counterfactual simulation of the driver's perfect legal lap. Genuine lost-control/contact events without gain, and substantial braking with a large speed loss without gain, get no race sanction. Braking does not forgive an advantage that remains.

Drivers have ten simulation seconds after rejoining to give back estimated time through a safe lift, or return specified positions. One incident does not produce both an ordinary strike and an advantage penalty. Failure to comply gives 10 seconds (5 with mitigation), or enough additional time to exceed a larger estimated shortcut gain. Reaching the finish settles outstanding reviews and includes time penalties in classification. Recovery preserves sanctions and adds five seconds. Separate normal infringements accumulate warnings; fourth and subsequent strikes add five seconds each.

Race control explains the outcome and the requested action on the HUD. Required advantage returns appear centrally with a countdown; routine notices stay out of the driving sightline. Solo results wait for remaining AI cars (up to 90 simulated seconds), allowing penalty-adjusted results instead of declaring the first road finisher the winner immediately.

## AI conduct
`ai-racecraft.js` separates tactical lane choice from path following. It can follow a tow on a straight, commit to the inside or alternate side, reject narrowing/occupied lanes, and reconsider a path when a rival's projected position conflicts. Aggression changes opportunity thresholds, not clearance requirements. A defender commits once per encounter rather than repeatedly blocking in reaction. Braking freezes tactical lateral offset while normal corner-following continues. Overlapped cars receive conservative lateral room; if both sides are blocked, the AI slows rather than forcing the gap. Advantage-return instructions override attack speed.

This is NOT a complete apex-entitlement steward. It conservatively grants space to overlap rather than weaponizing uncertainty about the front-axle/mirror tests. It does not yet classify every divebomb, assign collision blame, implement blue/yellow/safety-car flags, or prove collision-free racecraft against an unpredictable human. Those require additional scenario coverage and corner-phase tracking.

## Slipstream and DRS
`race-aero.js` computes a bounded tow from a nearby, aligned car ahead; distant, lateral, opposing-direction, finished or ghost cars do not contribute. Tow reduces drag by at most 12% and downforce by at most 5%. DRS reduces drag by 18% and load by 12%; these are game coefficients. Both humans and AI receive the same effects.

Detection uses interpolated passage timestamps, independent of driver update order. Race eligibility is latched at detection, not recalculated from a distance shortcut at activation. Press F or the standard gamepad's west face button once when ready to open DRS; press again to close. Braking or a sustained loaded turn closes DRS. An eligible driver can press again once straight and off the brakes; a small steering correction does not close the wing. A press before availability does not arm a later opening. In free practice, a nearby car is not required. DRS is currently authored only for Silverstone; other circuits explicitly show that zones are not mapped. Multiplayer clients send only the button request; the server decides eligibility and forces.

## Validation and remaining limits
Tests cover warning accumulation, incident deduplication, boundary flicker, two-wheel legality, braking/contact/loss-of-control mitigation, persistent gain despite braking, time repayment, returning positions, outstanding penalties at finish, DRS detection/order/braking/lap restrictions, slipstream alignment, defensive commitment, overlap room, shared physics and no player mutation by enhanced AI. Existing keyboard, tyre budget, stability, race, loading and multiplayer regressions also run.

Current track legality uses imported road-material sampling at wheel positions. It is not a surveyed white-line/contact-patch map; broad asphalt/runoff meshes can need authored legal boundaries before penalties can be called circuit-exact. Contact proximity is mitigation evidence, not fault attribution. The generic legal-time estimate can be imperfect at unusual corners. The Monaco chicane example is a design scenario, not a validated replay on a currently available Monaco model. Tune with recorded incidents and retain benefit of doubt; do not label this an exact F1 25 penalty system.

Side-by-side targets preserve an absolute corridor lane through the corner, accounting for yaw and wing sweep. A deterministic priority resolves close AI yielding to avoid mutual stops. Nose-to-tail traffic is handled separately so a stopped car does not prevent passing. Training by selection/evolution could later optimize offline pace, but is not implemented here and would still need separate collision, legality and traffic constraints.
